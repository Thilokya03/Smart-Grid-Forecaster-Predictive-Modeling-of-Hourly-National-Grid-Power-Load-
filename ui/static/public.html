A LITTLE CONTEXT

Understanding the forecast

National patterns. Hourly detail.
Estimates, with their limits in view.

What do MW and GW tell me?

These measure power: how much electricity the country is expected to need at a given time. 1 GW equals 1,000 MW. A block average describes the demand across its available hours, not the total electricity consumed.

What could I do with a lower-demand window?

For a flexible activity, it is a starting point for comparing possible times. The suggested window has the lowest average predicted national demand among the complete upcoming windows in your selection. It does not predict your home's usage, electricity price, or available local network capacity.

Why can actual demand be behind the forecast?

Source readings arrive after the period they describe. When there is a gap, the pipeline estimates the missing demand before producing the forecast. These nowcast values are estimates, not new NESO observations. New data can change the next published forecast.

How certain is the outlook?

Weather, behaviour, and unexpected events can change demand. The line is a point estimate; this publication does not provide calibrated uncertainty intervals. Longer horizons and gaps in observed demand deserve more caution. Higher demand here does not by itself indicate a shortage.
